The 2 August Wall: AI Act Article 50

Thursday, 23 July 2026 · ~8 min · Actionable Regulatory Intelligence

Ten days out, a cluster of hard deadlines converges on the same week. This episode walks through the six that matter most — from the EU AI Act's Article 50 transparency duties to California's Delete Act clock and FedRAMP Ready's retirement — plus France's under-15 social media ban, a $1.07B Medicaid deferral, and China's five-year IPv6 mandate.

Top Stories & Sources

1. EU AI Act — Article 50 transparency + GPAI enforcement (2 Aug)

Marking/disclosure duties for chatbots, synthetic media, emotion-recognition and biometric-categorisation systems begin 2 Aug 2026; Commission GPAI penalty powers (up to €15M / 3% turnover) switch on the same day. Article 50(2) marking-and-detection is deferred to 2 Dec 2026 for systems placed on the market before 2 Aug.

artificialintelligenceact.eu — Article 50
2. France — under-15 social media ban adopted (21 Jul)

Parliament definitively adopted the ban 21 Jul 2026 (Assembly 279–81). New accounts blocked from 1 Sep 2026, existing accounts from 1 Jan 2027. Adopted but not promulgated — gated on a likely Conseil constitutionnel referral and a TRIS standstill running to 10 Aug 2026 (EC detailed opinion issued 6 Jul).

Touteleurope.eu / Assemblée nationale (LCP)
3. California Delete Act (DROP) — 45-day duty binds (1 Aug)

Registered data brokers must process deletion requests received via the CPPA's DROP portal at least every 45 days from 1 Aug 2026. Calendar-driven, not release-driven.

cppa.ca.gov — DROP / Data Broker Registry
4. FedRAMP Ready retires (28 Jul)

No new Ready submissions after 28 Jul 2026; existing Ready listings convert to Legacy. Rev5 Ready holders must reach full Certification by the later of their next annual assessment expiry or 17 Nov 2026.

fedramp.gov
5. HHS/CMS defer ~$1.07B Medicaid matching funds (21 Jul)

~$867.5M deferred to California and $199M to Minnesota after focused financial reviews flagged high-risk claims. Deferrals, not permanent cuts; HHS signaled broader CMS/OIG exclusion authority.

hhs.gov — Newsroom (release 2026-07-21)
