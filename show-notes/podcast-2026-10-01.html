Borrowed Age Checks Don't Borrow The Liability

1 October 2026 · Actionable Regulatory Intelligence

Ofcom tests whether a device-level age signal can carry a legal duty with it. Plus Austria's NIS2 law binds today, China widens police cyber-inspection powers, the Fed opens its stablecoin rulebook, and the FDA elevates a heart-pump controller recall to Class I.

This week's stories

  1. Ofcom investigates Pornhub's age checks. Aylo relied on Apple's age signal from May 2026. Ofcom says the Online Safety Act puts effectiveness on the service provider “no matter at what stage an age check occurs,” and will not rule on how Apple operates its checks. Exposure: £18m or 10% of qualifying worldwide revenue.
    Ofcom, 23 September 2026
  2. Austria's NISG 2026 is in force. Obligations bind from today whether or not you have registered — the self-assessment is itself the duty. Registration via the Unternehmensserviceportal must initially be completed by 1 January 2027.
    USP (Austria) · NISG 2026 full text
  3. China's MPS Order No. 176 takes effect. Scope widens from “internet security” to “cyberspace security,” authorising online patrols, on-site checks and remote penetration testing of non-CII systems.
    Ministry of Public Security
  4. Fed's GENIUS Act stablecoin proposals published. Reserve backing, capital, custody, AML and a tailored approval pathway for Board-supervised payment stablecoin issuers. Comments close 30 November 2026.
    Federal Register, 29 September 2026
  5. FDA elevates Abiomed Impella controller recall to Class I. Purge-flag component failure; three deaths and 37 serious injuries as of 17 August 2026. Boston Scientific's Imager II catheter removal also elevated to Class I.
    FDA — Impella · FDA — Imager II
