Episode 39 — Connecticut's AI Law Binds Thursday

CyberEyeQ Weekly · Thursday, 24 September 2026 · Actionable Regulatory Intelligence

Connecticut's CART Act has its first effective date in seven days, and almost every summary in circulation — including the Attorney General's own consumer guidance — puts the wrong duties on that date. We read the enacted text section by section. Plus Ireland's €403 million Google fine, a $2 million NIST 800-171 settlement, and an IVDR cliff on Saturday.

This week's stories

  1. Connecticut — CART Act first tranche commences 1 October. Content provenance for genAI providers above one million monthly users (Sec. 15), written acceptance for AI subscriptions (Sec. 1), a WARN-notice AI disclosure (Sec. 26), and an evidentiary rule stripping employers of the automated-tool defence (Secs 13–14). The chatbot duties are January 2027; the AEDT disclosure package is October 2027.
    Public Act 26-15 — enacted text
  2. EU — IVDR class C legacy transition closes 26 September. Under IVDR Art. 110(3c) as inserted by Regulation (EU) 2024/1860, a signed notified-body agreement is required by 26 September 2026; the application had to be lodged by 26 May 2026 and the QMS in place by 26 May 2025.
    Regulation (EU) 2024/1860
  3. Italy — D.Lgs. 160/2026 in force 30 September. Adapts Italian law to the EU AI Act on AI in policing and on civil and criminal liability. Clause-level commentary is unverified against the decree's own articles.
    Normattiva — D.Lgs. 9 settembre 2026, n. 160
  4. Ireland — DPC fines Google €403 million. Location-data processing 2018–2020; compliance ordered within six months.
    Data Protection Commission
  5. US — DOJ settles cyber-fraud claims for $2,042,518. False Claims Act allegations over NIST SP 800-171 non-compliance on a Department of Defense contract network; relator share $375,823. Allegations only.
    Department of Justice
